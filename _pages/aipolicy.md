## **Policy on Use of AI Tools**

In this graduate-level course, our goal is to help you to acquire
high-level knowledge and skills about mobile and pervasive system
design and implementation.  While the hands-on projects involve
programming, we are not trying to teach you how to write code — we
assume that you already have good programming skills before you take
this class.  It is the high-level synthesis of technical concepts and
low-level software components that is our focus.

With this in mind, we take a very liberal policy towards use of AI
tools.  You are welcome to use any AI tool that you have access to.
All that we ask is that you acknowledge explicitly and clearly (a)
which tool(s) you have used in specific coursework, and (b) how you
used that tool.  These can be just a few sentences in the slides that
you use for project checkpoints and commscans, and in the poster you
prepare for the final demo and poster session.  Be safe and document
your use clearly and completely.  ***Not properly acknowledging use of
AI tools constitutes an AIV.***
    
There is one particular aspect of the course that requires additional
clarification.  The ability to read original research papers,
understand them in context, and extract their essence is a big part of
what this course is trying to teach.  Therefore, we expect you to read
and understand the research papers without the use of AI.  If you
short-circuit this process by only reading an AI-generated summary of
the paper, you are violating the intent of the course.  That would be
an AIV.  The correct way to use an AI tool relative to the readings is
as follows::

* First, read each paper fully and write your own half-page summary of
  it.

* Afterwards you may compare your summary with one generated by an AI
  tool.

* Use the gap as a teacher.  How much overlap is there between the two
  summaries?  Did you miss some important things that the AI summary
  contains?  Did the AI tool miss some things that you thought
  important?  Why do you think they were missed?

* ***Do not look at the AI summary until you have completely read and
  understood the full paper.  Violating this rule is an AIV.***  Of
  course, not using AI summaries at all is perfectly fine.
